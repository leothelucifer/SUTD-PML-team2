## Essential data facts for this ML project

| Area | What matters |
|---|---|
| **Dataset** | PhysioNet 2019: **40,336 ICU records, 1,552,210 hourly rows**, 40 inputs and one label. Records are stays—not necessarily unique people. Hours within a stay are correlated. |
| **Training data** | Current split: **11,183 records / 435,449 hours**. Longer stays contribute more rows, so assess both hourly and record-level statistics. |
| **Target** | `SepsisLabel` is **already shifted six hours early** and stays positive afterward. Never shift it again. Predicting it does not automatically demonstrate early-warning benefit or clinical risk. |
| **Imbalance** | Only **2.16% of training hours are positive**. Predicting zero always gives about 97.84% accuracy—use precision–recall metrics, not accuracy alone. |
| **Missingness** | HR **7.77%**, temperature **66.22%**, lactate **96.53%**, EtCO₂ **100%** missing in training. Missing is not zero or normal; entirely unobserved measurements cannot provide a learned value effect. |
| **Preprocessing** | Preserve raw data. Use record-local, past-only forward-fill and training-fitted fallback/scaling. Compare missingness and measurement-age features on/off: they may capture care practices rather than physiology. |
| **Data quality** | Check units, extreme values, recording gaps and stay lengths. Do not automatically clip or remove unusual values. `ICULOS` is an observation index; recorded history may not cover the complete stay. |
| **Splits** | Source A: approximately **55/15/15/15 train/calibration/validation/internal test**. All B is external test. Keep every stay together; record-disjoint does not guarantee person-disjoint. |
| **Leakage** | At hour *t*, use only current/past information. Exclude future values, backfill, whole-stay summaries, labels, IDs and source identifiers from model inputs. Verify prefix invariance. |
| **Evaluation** | Fit on training, calibrate on calibration, select models/thresholds on validation. Freeze decisions before testing. Report AP, AUROC, Brier/calibration, early-detection recall, false-alert burden and record-grouped uncertainty. |
| **Transfer and timing** | B measures cross-source transfer. Do not retune on B. Distinguish hourly decisions from cooldown notifications; inferred onset and lead time have benchmark limitations, especially for first-row-positive stays. |
| **Reproducibility and privacy** | Track hashes, versions, seeds and splits. Keep data in Git-ignored `data/`; clear patient-level notebook outputs. Resolve license terms before redistribution. Synthetic scores are software checks—not real-data or clinical evidence. |

**Success means:** leakage-free, reproducible predictions that detect useful early signals with an acceptable alert burden—and retain performance on untouched source B.
